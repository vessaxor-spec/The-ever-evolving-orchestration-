# The Constitution of TEO

This document defines the enduring principles of The Ever-Evolving Orchestration.

Policies, registries, implementations, and evaluations may change frequently. This Constitution should change only when the philosophy of the project changes.

## Article I: Architecture before implementation

The architecture is more important than any individual implementation.

Implementations change. The decision structure should endure.

## Article II: No permanent provider preference

No provider receives permanent preference.

Every routing decision should be justified by current evidence, declared constraints, and the requirements of the task.

## Article III: Capabilities precede implementations

Implementations provide capabilities.

Routing decisions should identify required capabilities before selecting a provider, model, agent, tool, or service.

## Article IV: Important decisions must be explainable

A consequential routing decision should preserve enough information for a human reviewer to understand why it was made.

If a policy cannot explain its decision, its use should be reconsidered.

## Article V: Evidence outranks opinion

Reproducible evaluations, measurements, documented trade-offs, and primary sources should outweigh reputation, novelty, and personal preference.

## Article VI: Verification follows consequence

Verification effort should reflect the risk, reversibility, and potential impact of the decision.

High-consequence execution should not rely only on self-verification by the same implementation that performed the work.

## Article VII: Evolution through stewardship

TEO evolves through stewardship.

Stewards are responsible for improving the project without weakening its principles, precision, public scope, or usefulness to implementers.

## Article VIII: History matters

Major decisions should preserve enough context for future stewards to understand what changed, why it changed, and what alternatives were considered.

## Article IX: Public by design

TEO is intended for public use.

The project must not depend on private prompts, confidential systems, proprietary workflows, hidden benchmarks, personal data, or employer-specific information.

## Article X: Complexity must justify itself

Every major addition must improve decision quality, reduce meaningful risk, improve interoperability, or clarify implementation.

Complexity without a measurable purpose does not belong in TEO.

## Article XI: Prepared for change

Every decision should leave the system better prepared for change than it was before.
