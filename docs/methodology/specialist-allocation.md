# Specialist Allocation Method

TEO allocates specialists after resolving the primary responsibility and before selecting an implementation.

## Purpose

The specialist layer allows domain expertise to grow without turning model names, providers, or private agent prompts into architecture.

## Allocation sequence

1. Mission Control classifies the task and risk.
2. Mission Control selects the primary core team.
3. The primary team selects a stable worker binding.
4. The specialist registry is searched for a matching domain specialist.
5. Authority, professional boundaries, risk, and access are checked.
6. Supporting teams and verification are assigned.
7. The implementation is resolved through the normal routing policy.

## Allocation criteria

A specialist is eligible only when:

- its primary team matches the responsibility owner
- its worker binding matches the task capability
- its domain description matches the requested work
- its boundaries permit the requested action
- its risk profile can be satisfied by the assigned verification path
- required tools, evidence, authorization, and human approvals are available

## Multiple specialists

Mission Control may activate multiple specialists when a task crosses domains. One specialist remains primary for each bounded work unit. Handoffs must identify which specialist produced each claim, decision, or artifact.

## Independence

A specialist may contribute to planning, execution, review, or verification, but a single specialist must not become the sole authority across all stages of consequential work.

## Credit

The 56-role specialist roster was created by Sylvester Roxas. Every public role card and registry entry preserves that attribution.

## Validation

The registry must pass these checks before release:

- exactly 56 unique specialist identifiers
- every specialist has one primary team
- every specialist has a worker binding
- every specialist has a risk profile
- every specialist has a public role card
- every role card contains the creator credit
- every high-risk or critical specialist has independent verification requirements
- no specialist bypasses Mission Control or core-team ownership
